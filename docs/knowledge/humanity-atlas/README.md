# ATLAS Humanity Atlas

## Purpose

ATLAS Humanity Atlas is the governed, cross-module knowledge domain for research about human life, health, disease, recovery, society, history, technology, culture, spirituality, survival, and the interaction between these fields.

This material belongs primarily to **Knowledge Atlas**. Domain-specific claims are linked outward to the owning ATLAS module rather than duplicated as separate sources of truth. Health-related material integrates with **ATLAS Health / Health Frontiers**; historical and religious material can be referenced by **ATLAS Bible OS** where appropriate; economics, law, education, security, environment, and technology connect to their respective domain modules.

## Core knowledge model

Every topic should be represented with its counterpart and interventions instead of as an isolated fact.

Examples:

- Health → Disease / Injury → Risk factors → Prevention → Diagnosis → Treatment / Remedy → Rehabilitation / Recovery → Cure when evidence supports that term → Limitations / uncertainty.
- Knowledge → Error / uncertainty → Verification → Correction → Current confidence.
- Safety → Hazard / threat → Prevention → Detection → Response → Recovery / mitigation.
- Wealth → Scarcity / poverty → Causes → Prevention / resilience → Assistance / intervention → Sustainable recovery.
- Peace → Conflict / violence → Risk factors → Prevention → Protection → Resolution → Reconciliation.
- Learning → Ignorance / misinformation → Education → Practice → Assessment → Updating.

No ATLAS surface may imply that every problem has a cure or definitive solution. The system must distinguish cure, treatment, management, rehabilitation, prevention, risk reduction, hypothesis, tradition, and insufficient evidence.

## Time span

The knowledge map should support comparisons from antiquity, with emphasis from the historical period of Jesus of Nazareth (1st century CE), through medieval and early-modern periods, the scientific and industrial revolutions, modern medicine, and the present day.

Historical, scientific, theological, and faith claims must retain separate truth-state labels. Historical religious sources may be analyzed academically without converting theological claims into scientific findings or scientific findings into theological conclusions.

## Human life-course map

The project should organize knowledge around the human life course:

1. Conception and prenatal development
2. Birth and neonatal life
3. Infancy
4. Childhood
5. Adolescence
6. Adulthood
7. Reproduction and family life
8. Work, society, relationships, culture, and environment
9. Disease, injury, treatment, recovery, disability, and adaptation
10. Aging and geriatrics
11. Palliative care, dying, death, bereavement, and legacy

Cross-cutting layers include genetics, anatomy, physiology, microbiology, immunology, neuroscience, psychology, nutrition, public health, environment, economics, law, education, technology, ethics, philosophy, religion, community, safety, emergency preparedness, and sustainability.

## Initial research seed — 2026-09-06

A multidisciplinary deep-research pass was commissioned to build an expandable "Atlas of knowledge about humanity" rather than a one-off article. Its scope includes:

- origins and evolution of Homo sapiens;
- life-course development from conception through death;
- health, disease, prevention, diagnosis, treatment, recovery, rehabilitation, and evidence-supported cures;
- modern, traditional, and complementary medicine with evidence grading;
- psychology, psychiatry, trauma, EMDR, cognition, and resilience;
- sociology, anthropology, culture, religion, philosophy, ethics, and epistemology;
- economics, work, institutions, law, governance, conflict, cooperation, and human rights;
- technology, engineering, computing, AI, communications, food, water, energy, housing, transport, and cities;
- climate, ecology, sustainability, environmental hazards, emergencies, and disaster resilience;
- practical survival and thriving: prevention, hygiene, food and water safety, activity, sleep, social connection, first aid, financial literacy, lifelong learning, critical thinking, privacy, cybersecurity, preparedness, aging, and end-of-life planning.

The research compares what people believed or practiced in earlier periods with later evidence, identifies major advances, notes failed or harmful practices, and preserves open questions instead of inventing certainty.

## Evidence and provenance rules

All future entries should preserve:

- source and publication date;
- source type (guideline, systematic review, trial, observational study, historical source, theological source, textbook, policy document, etc.);
- evidence strength / confidence;
- domain owner;
- historical period;
- jurisdiction where relevant;
- whether the item is current, superseded, disputed, experimental, traditional, or insufficiently supported;
- last verification date;
- links to the original record rather than a shadow copy when an authoritative ATLAS record already exists.

For medicine and health, prioritize systematic reviews, meta-analyses, clinical guidelines, public-health agencies, scientific academies, peer-reviewed literature, and clearly labeled experimental evidence. Traditional practices should be studied but never presented as effective merely because they are old or widely used.

## ATLAS integration

**Primary owner:** Knowledge Atlas

**Secondary integrations:**

- ATLAS Health / Health Frontiers — biomedical, clinical, public-health, therapeutic, life-course, aging, and palliative evidence.
- ATLAS Bible OS — historical and theological context, with faith claims explicitly separated from empirical claims.
- Finance / Accounting / Enterprise — economic life, work, household resilience, institutions, and resource management.
- Education — curricula, professional disciplines, lifelong learning, and knowledge maps.
- Security — personal safety, violence prevention, emergency response, risk, privacy, and cybersecurity.
- Analytics / AI Core — evidence ranking, comparisons, provenance, contradiction detection, timelines, and cross-domain synthesis.

Knowledge Atlas remains the retrieval and provenance layer; it must not become a duplicate database for records already owned by another module.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent metrics, cures, diagnoses, historical facts, or scientific consensus.
- Do not call a condition "cured" unless the evidence and accepted terminology support it.
- Do not substitute spiritual or historical practices for appropriate medical care.
- Do not erase faith perspectives; label them correctly and keep them distinguishable from empirical evidence.
- Preserve tenant, RBAC, consent, audit, and provenance boundaries when this knowledge is connected to user or patient data.
- Prefer canonical ATLAS module APIs and records over duplicated storage.
