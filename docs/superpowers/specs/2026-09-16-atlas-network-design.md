# ATLAS Network — Product, Pricing, Finance, and Compensation Design

**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Status:** Approved design, pending implementation-plan review  
**Owner module:** Business Suite  
**Secondary integrations:** CRM, Finance, Accounting, ATLAS Pay, Identity/RBAC, Audit, Analytics, Tax  
**Canonical repository:** `atlasenterprisesuite/atlasenterprisesuite`

## 1. Purpose

ATLAS Network is a multinational referral and partner-distribution subsystem inside ATLAS Enterprise Suite. It is not a standalone application and must not create a parallel source of truth for customers, organizations, identity, accounting, payments, or analytics.

The system exists to let customers recommend ATLAS products and let approved partners earn compensation from verified customer revenue while preserving margin, auditability, tenant isolation, payment traceability, and compliance controls.

The economic model is sales-led rather than recruitment-led:

- partner enrollment is free;
- no compensation is paid merely for enrolling another partner;
- no rank is purchased;
- commissions arise only from eligible, collected customer revenue;
- returns, refunds, chargebacks, taxes, credits, and other exclusions reduce the commissionable base;
- all earnings representations generated by ATLAS must be backed by available evidence and approved disclosure rules.

## 2. Product positioning

ATLAS Network will live under `Business -> ATLAS Network` and reuse the existing Business Suite, CRM, Finance, authentication, organization, audit, and provider-integration patterns.

Primary user classes:

1. **Customer** — buys ATLAS products or subscriptions and may optionally refer others.
2. **Partner** — receives a referral identity and may earn commissions on eligible verified sales.
3. **Partner Leader** — a partner who qualifies for a higher operating rank through verified customer-production and retention metrics.
4. **Organization Finance/Admin** — manages price books, commission rules, liabilities, payout approvals, reserves, and financial reporting.
5. **Compliance/Admin** — manages earnings-claim controls, policy breaches, payout holds, and partner eligibility.
6. **Platform Auditor** — read-only access to immutable evidence, rule versions, calculations, and payout history.

A user may be both customer and partner, but customer purchases must remain distinguishable from third-party customer sales for reporting and qualification purposes.

### 3.2 Price-book rules

Each sellable SKU must reference a versioned price book containing:

- country or commercial region;
- transaction currency;
- base USD reference price;
- local list price;
- effective start and end timestamps;
- tax treatment code;
- commissionability flag;
- maximum commission-rate override where product margin requires a lower cap;
- approval actor and audit event.

Historical transactions retain the exact price-book version used at checkout. A later price edit must never restate an earlier order or commission calculation.

### 3.3 FX policy

Every non-USD transaction persists:

- transaction amount and currency;
- captured FX rate and FX source identifier;
- captured-at timestamp;
- translated USD amount for corporate reporting;
- settlement amount where different;
- realized FX difference after settlement where applicable.

Commission calculations are performed from the transaction's immutable commissionable base in the payout ledger currency defined for that partner and rule version. Recalculation from a newer spot rate is prohibited.

## 4. Commissionable Net Revenue

`Commissionable Net Revenue (CNR)` is the only revenue base that may fund commissions.

For an order or invoice line:

`CNR = cash_collected - sales_and_indirect_taxes - refunds - chargebacks - credits - noncommissionable_pass_through_fees`

Rules:

- pending authorization is not collected revenue;
- unpaid invoices generate no payable commission;
- refunded revenue produces a negative commission adjustment against the same rule version that generated the original commission;
- chargebacks create a partner receivable or future-offset balance when the original payout has already settled;
- internal transfers, promotional credits, tax components, and partner enrollment create no CNR;
- customer self-purchases may be tracked separately and must not be counted as third-party retail production.

## 5. Compensation model

### 5.1 Maximum pool

For each eligible CNR event:

`Commission Pool Cap = min(20% of CNR, 35% of contribution_margin)`

Contribution margin is calculated after direct product/service delivery costs and provider pass-through costs defined by Finance. A product may configure a lower product-specific commission cap.

The commission engine must reject or proportionally constrain any rule set whose aggregate potential payout exceeds the cap.

### 5.2 Launch allocation

Initial maximum allocation by eligible customer sale:

| Component | Maximum rate of eligible CNR |
| --- | ---: |
| Direct originating partner | 12.0% |
| Level 2 customer-sales override | 3.0% |
| Level 3 customer-sales override | 1.5% |
| Leadership pool | 1.5% |
| Authorized campaign incentive pool | 2.0% |
| **Absolute aggregate maximum** | **20.0%** |

The engine pays only the components that actually qualify. Unused pool is retained by the company and is never automatically redistributed.

### 5.3 Prohibited compensation triggers

No commission, bonus, rank credit, or payout may be generated solely from:

- enrolling or recruiting a partner;
- paying an enrollment fee;
- purchasing a rank;
- mandatory inventory loading;
- buying training to qualify;
- making a non-customer internal transfer;
- creating a synthetic or duplicate customer account;
- a refunded, reversed, fraudulent, or unpaid sale.

## 6. Partner ranks

Initial operating ranks:

1. `partner`
2. `builder`
3. `leader`
4. `director`
5. `global_ambassador`

Ranks are computed from versioned qualification policies, not manually purchased.

Each policy may use:

- active third-party customers;
- trailing CNR;
- retention rate;
- refund and chargeback rate;
- compliance standing;
- organization depth with qualifying customer production;
- training or policy acknowledgements that carry no purchase requirement.

A rank downgrade must preserve historical rank snapshots on prior commission events. Manual rank overrides require privileged permission, a reason code, effective dates, and an audit event.

## 7. Attribution model

Every partner receives one or more signed referral identifiers. Supported acquisition channels may include link, QR, campaign, salesperson-assisted checkout, or approved CRM attribution.

Attribution records must contain:

- referring partner ID;
- customer/contact ID;
- channel and campaign metadata;
- first-touch timestamp;
- qualifying conversion timestamp;
- attribution policy version;
- anti-fraud status;
- immutable evidence references.

The launch design uses deterministic attribution. The implementation must define one authoritative conversion owner per eligible sale and must not create duplicate commissions from multiple attribution systems.

## 11. Finance and accounting integration

Commission events create financial liabilities only after the underlying CNR becomes eligible under the active settlement policy.

Minimum accounting flow:

1. Customer payment is recorded through the existing commerce/payment source.
2. Eligible CNR is calculated from immutable source values.
3. Commission events are calculated using the effective rule version.
4. Finance recognizes commission expense and partner payable according to the configured accounting policy.
5. Refunds and chargebacks create reversing commission events.
6. Approved payout batches clear partner payable against the payment/settlement account.
7. Provider fees and FX gains/losses are recorded separately from commission expense.

The Network UI must not fabricate a paid status. A payout is `paid` only when the configured provider or accounting settlement source confirms settlement.

## 12. Payout lifecycle

Allowed payout states:

`accruing -> pending_review -> approved -> processing -> paid`

Exception states:

`held`, `failed`, `reversed`, `cancelled`

Partner balances must distinguish:

- estimated;
- pending/locked;
- available;
- held;
- paid;
- reversed/offset.

The UI must explain that estimated earnings are not payable cash until all eligibility and settlement conditions are satisfied.

No production payout provider is assumed. The system must implement to the real provider boundary and show configuration state if no authorized provider is connected.

## 13. Compliance controls

### 13.1 Earnings Claims Guard

ATLAS-generated partner content must not publish unsupported income claims.

The guard evaluates proposed earnings content against:

- approved disclosure templates;
- current verified earnings-distribution data where available;
- prohibited claim patterns;
- jurisdictional policy version;
- content-approval status.

If evidence is insufficient, ATLAS blocks publication from its own tooling and explains the reason. The system must not substitute fabricated averages or lifestyle claims.

### 13.2 Fraud and integrity controls

Flag at minimum:

- duplicate identity or payout-account patterns;
- self-referral abuse;
- circular referral relationships;
- abnormal refund/chargeback rates;
- rapid synthetic-account creation;
- commission events without source transactions;
- changed attribution after conversion without privileged audited override.

Flags do not automatically imply wrongdoing. They produce reviewable compliance events and may place payouts on hold according to policy.

### 13.3 Legal launch gate

Before public commercial launch in a jurisdiction, ATLAS must require confirmation that the applicable compensation plan, customer terms, partner agreement, earnings disclosures, privacy terms, tax handling, payout/KYC requirements, and marketing rules have been reviewed and approved for that jurisdiction by authorized company counsel or compliance leadership.

The product must not represent this software design as legal approval.

## 14. Analytics

Minimum metrics:

- active customers;
- active partners;
- MRR and ARR attributable to Network;
- gross revenue and CNR;
- commission expense;
- commission expense as % of CNR;
- contribution margin before and after commissions;
- partner/customer conversion rate;
- 30/90/365-day customer retention;
- churn;
- refund and chargeback rates;
- CAC where acquisition spend exists;
- LTV when sufficient cohort data exists;
- partner activation and productivity distributions;
- payout holds and reversals;
- country/currency exposure.

Empty datasets show empty or configuration states. No sample production metrics may appear as real data.

## 15. Primary user flows

### Customer referral conversion

`Partner -> referral link/QR -> customer landing/checkout -> attribution -> payment -> CNR -> commission event -> hold/availability -> payout`

### Partner enrollment

`Authenticated user -> Partner enrollment -> terms/policy acknowledgement -> eligibility checks -> partner code -> referral tools`

There is no paid enrollment step.

### Pricing administration

`Finance Admin -> price books -> create/version price -> validate overlap/currency/tax fields -> approve -> future effective activation -> audit event`

Past prices remain immutable.

### Commission-rule administration

`Finance/Compliance Admin -> draft rule version -> simulate against validation fixtures -> verify 20% CNR and 35% contribution-margin caps -> approve -> future effective activation -> audit event`

Historical commission events retain the rule version originally used.

### Payout approval

`Eligible commission events -> payout batch -> compliance/finance review -> authorized approval -> provider processing -> provider settlement confirmation -> paid status -> accounting clearing entry`

## 16. Error and state handling

The module must explicitly handle:

- unauthenticated user;
- authenticated user without organization access;
- insufficient permission;
- missing price book;
- overlapping active price-book windows;
- missing FX capture for non-base currency;
- missing contribution-margin data;
- commission rule exceeding cap;
- missing source transaction;
- refund or chargeback reversal;
- partner payout hold;
- provider unavailable or not configured;
- failed payout;
- empty analytics period;
- stale or superseded pricing/rule version.

Sensitive calculation failures fail closed: no commission or payout is released until the source data and rule evaluation are valid.

## 18. Deployment gates

Production launch requires all of the following:

1. Database migration reviewed and applied through the established Supabase workflow.
2. RLS and permission tests pass.
3. Price books and commission rules have approved effective versions.
4. Accounting mappings are configured.
5. Country/legal launch gate is approved for each enabled jurisdiction.
6. Payout provider is configured, or payout execution remains visibly disabled.
7. Earnings Claims Guard has an approved policy set.
8. Refund/chargeback reversal flow is verified.
9. Audit logs are queryable for privileged actions.
10. Typecheck, automated tests, production build, and affected-route verification pass.
11. No credentials, provider secrets, or private tax/bank data are committed to the repository.
12. Production telemetry can distinguish calculation failures, payout failures, and compliance holds without exposing sensitive data.

## 19. Implementation boundaries

This design authorizes implementation of the ATLAS Network software foundation and launch price book. It does not authorize:

- automatic public marketing claims about partner income;
- live payouts without an authorized provider and required identity/tax controls;
- country launch without the legal/compliance gate;
- fabricated customers, revenue, metrics, provider connections, or paid states;
- destructive modification of existing Business, CRM, Finance, or Accounting data models when an additive integration can preserve compatibility.

## 20. Success criteria

ATLAS Network is successful when a real eligible customer transaction can be attributed to a partner, converted into immutable CNR, evaluated under a versioned compensation rule, represented as an auditable commission liability, reversed correctly when the source economics reverse, and included in a controlled payout workflow — all without breaking tenant isolation or inventing external-provider success.

The commercial design is successful when the company can see both partner earnings and the company's post-commission contribution margin for every product, country, cohort, and payout period.
